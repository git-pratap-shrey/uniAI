# Digital Forensics Life Cycle

**Subject**: CYBER_SECURITY | **Unit**: unit4

## Definition
The Digital Forensics Life Cycle is a structured, legal procedure involving the preparation, collection, examination, and presentation of digital evidence in a legal context.

## Key points
- Phases: 1. Preparation/Identification, 2. Collection/Recording, 3. Storing/Transporting, 4. Examination/Investigation, 5. Analysis/Interpretation, 6. Reporting, 7. Testifying.
- Identification: Recognize the incident, obtain search warrants, and identify relevant evidence (files, logs, processes).
- Preservation: Secure evidence and protect integrity using write-blocking tools and cryptographic hashes.
- Analysis: Reconstruct data fragments, draw conclusions, and use tools like FTK or EnCase.
- Reporting: Document findings in oral or written form, including case identifiers and examiner signatures.

## Important terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Write-blocking | Tool ensuring no data is added to the suspect device during imaging. |
| Cryptographic Hash | Used to verify if evidence files have been altered. |
