# Intellectual Property Rights

**Subject**: CYBER_SECURITY | **Unit**: unit5

## Definition
Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the mind—innovations, inventions, artistic works, symbols, and images used in commerce—protected by law through patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secrets.

## Key points
- Patents: Protect inventions (products/processes) for typically 20 years; require novelty, non-obviousness, and utility.
- Copyrights: Protect original creative works (books, music, software); duration is life of author + 60 years.
- Trademarks: Protect brand identifiers (logos, slogans); must be unique, distinctive, and non-deceptive.
- Trade Secrets: Confidential business information (formulas, methods) that provide a competitive advantage.
- Common IP issues: Copyright infringement, trademark infringement, trade secret theft, patent infringement, counterfeiting, and plagiarism.
- Protection strategies: Registering rights, using encryption, monitoring online activity, and taking legal action.

## Important terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Patent | Exclusive rights granted for an invention |
| Copyright | Exclusive rights for original works of authorship |
| Trademark | Distinctive sign identifying goods or services |
